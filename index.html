Case study · OutLoud

From a 33‑person survey to 60 real users.

My Airtribe capstone, run like a real launch: research first, the prompt as the spec, and a funnel read without spin.

01

The problem

They can write it. They can't say it. Job seekers and early-career workers who read and write English well have no affordable, private, daily way to practise speaking it. Coaching is expensive and public, apps teach grammar, and free content is passive.

27+
of 33 surveyed want to practise with an AI
15+
of 33 quit past attempts from inconsistency, not cost
7+
of 33 name being judged as their single biggest fear

Survey and interviews, n=33: 18 working professionals, 14 active job seekers, 1 student.

02

Insight → decision

  • “Log judge karenge.”

    Being watched is the barrier, not vocabulary.

    SoVoice-only and private. The AI never mentions accent, and no score goes below 40.

  • “Answer pata hone ke bawajood bol nahi paata.”

    The words exist. They just don't come out.

    SoThree questions that ramp: an easy win, a real story, then an opinion. Feedback ends with a “say it better” rewrite you can hear.

  • “Consistency maintain nahi hui.”

    Motivation isn't what fails. Consistency is.

    SoFour-to-five-minute sessions, a streak tracker, score trend and session history built to bring people back.

03

The prompt is the spec

  • Two model calls per session, not one per question. Questions are generated once up front and scored once at the end, which keeps cost and wait time flat.
  • Every AI call has a fallback. A local question bank if Gemini fails, the browser's own voice if Sarvam fails. A session never dies on a 500.
  • Only text reaches the model. The browser's speech engine returns a transcript. OutLoud never records or stores audio, and Gemini only ever sees the words.
  • Reliability is a tracked metric. 84% of sessions were scored by the real model rather than the fallback.
04

What the data says

The biggest leak happens before any AI runs. 39 of 60 users never got past the mic permission prompt. Of the 21 who did, 14 finished a full session. That's two in three, so the core loop works once people are in.

North Star, week-4 completion (target 40%). Needs a clean 28-day cohort. Not computed, not guessed.
05

Go-to-market & next

Distribution was hands-on: WhatsApp communities, one-to-one outreach and LinkedIn. Every user who tried it got a personal follow-up 3–4 days later, and every non-return became another research conversation.

  • Reminder notificationtargets the Day-2 drop-off
  • Live conversationreal back-and-forth, capped length
  • Skill trackinggrowth per dimension, not one score
  • Cross-device syncno lost streaks
  • Streak badgesopt-in D7, D15 milestones, never scores
  • Resume-based questionspersonalised practice
